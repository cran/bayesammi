1 Abstract

Crossa et al. (2011) fitted the Bayesian AMMI model with two bilinear terms to the maize trial Maiz. They ran two chains of 35000 iterations, discarded a burn-in of 5000 and thinned by 2. This vignette repeats that run and compares the posterior summaries with their Tables 1 and B1. The overall mean, the error variance, the main effects, the singular values and the scores of the first term agree within Monte Carlo error. The second scores differ, for a reason the paper does not discuss: the sign of a weak term.

3.1 The error variance

The paper reports a prior scale of \(S_0 = 10^8\) with \(\nu = 1\), but its posterior of \(\sigma^2\) (mean 763600) is what a vague prior gives. Taken literally, with \(S^2_0 = 10^8\), the prior would add \(10^8/721\) to the posterior mean:

Here the means differ, mostly in sign, and the published means are smaller in absolute value. The second term is weak: the HPD interval of \(\lambda_2\) reaches down to near zero, both in the paper (2.09) and here (13.07). A chain without sign alignment then moves between the two mirror images \((\va_2, \vg_2)\) and \((-\va_2, -\vg_2)\). Its posterior means are averages over both images and shrink towards zero:

The published value, 0.2483, is smaller in absolute value than the aligned posterior mean, -0.436, and has the opposite sign. A chain that spends a share \(q\) of its run in the image opposite to the least-squares orientation has a mean of about \((1 - 2q)\) times the aligned one. The published value corresponds to \(q \approx\) 0.78: the published chain spent most of its run in the other image. The aligned summaries in this package describe one orientation of the term, and the scores of genotypes then keep their meaning relative to each other.
