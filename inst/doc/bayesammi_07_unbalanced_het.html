1 Abstract

Real trials lose plots, and some genotypes are not grown in every environment. The error variance also differs from one site to the next. This vignette shows how bayes_ammi() handles both: missing plots and empty cells by data augmentation, and a separate error variance for every environment with .het = TRUE. It ends with a comparison of the two variance models on Maiz.

2 Missing plots and empty cells

2.1 The idea

With \(B\) the largest number of plots in a cell, a cell \((i, j)\) with \(n_{ij} < B\) plots has \(B - n_{ij}\) missing plots. A cell with \(n_{ij} = 0\) is an empty cell. At every iteration the sampler draws the missing plots from the model, \[y^{\mathrm{mis}}_{ijl} \mid \vtheta \sim N(\eta_{ij}, \sigma^2_j), \qquad \eta_{ij} = \mu + \tau_i + \delta_j + \sum_{k=1}^{K}\lambda_k\alpha_{ik}\gamma_{jk},\] and then draws the parameters from the conditional distributions of the balanced design (vignette 2) given the completed data. This is the data augmentation of Tanner and Wong (1987). The draws of the parameters come from their posterior given the observed plots only, so nothing is invented: a missing plot adds no information, and an empty cell is predicted from the row and column structure of the model. The missing plots are assumed to be missing at random (Little and Rubin 2019).

The sampler needs only the sums, sums of squares and counts of the observed plots in every cell, so the completed data are never stored.

3 Environment-specific error variances

3.1 The model

With .het = TRUE the plots of environment \(j\) have their own variance, \(e_{ijl} \sim N(0, \sigma^2_j)\), and every \(\sigma^2_j\) has the scaled inverse chi-squared prior of the common variance. Write \(w_j = 1/\sigma^2_j\). The conditional distributions change as follows.

  • \(\sigma^2_j\): scaled inverse chi-squared with \(\nu + BG\) degrees of freedom and the residual sum of squares of environment \(j\).
  • \(\tau_i\): the data precision becomes \(B\sum_j w_j\), the same for every genotype.
  • \(\mu\) and \(\delta_j\): no longer independent, so \((\mu, \mathbf{d})\) is drawn jointly from its multivariate normal conditional.
  • \(\lambda_k\): mean and variance weighted by \(w_j\).
  • \(\va_k\): still von Mises-Fisher, because the quadratic term \(\sum_j w_j\gamma_{jk}^2\) does not depend on \(\va_k\).
  • \(\vg_k\): the quadratic term \(\sum_j w_j\gamma_{jk}^2\) now varies on the sphere. The conditional is a Bingham-von Mises-Fisher distribution (Hoff 2009), drawn one coordinate at a time in the eigenbasis of the quadratic form by slice sampling.

Perez-Elizalde et al. (2012) give the general linear-bilinear model with these conditionals.

The environment-specific variances lower WAIC by 124, 5.5 times the standard error of the difference. They also sharpen the interaction: the lower HPD limit of \(\lambda_2\) rises from 64 to 910, because the plots of the noisy environments no longer blur the second term.
