1 Abstract

A posterior sample answers the questions a breeder asks in probabilities: how likely is a genotype to be among the best, how stable is it, and what yield can be expected in a cell that was never grown. This vignette goes through bam_superiority(), bam_ranks(), bam_stability(), bam_predict() and bam_winners(), and through the model choice tools bam_compare() and bam_ppc().

fit <-
  bayes_ammi(
      .data   = Maiz
    , .y      = y
    , .gen    = entry
    , .env    = site
    , .rep    = rep
    , .nIter  = 4000
    , .k      = 2
    , .burnin = 1000
    , .thin   = 1
    , .chains = 2
    , .priors = bam_priors(
          mu_mean     = 0
        , mu_var      = 1e+8
        , tau_mean    = 0
        , tau_var     = 1e+8
        , delta_mean  = 0
        , delta_var   = 1e+8
        , lambda_mean = 0
        , lambda_var  = 1e+8
        , nu          = 1
        , s2          = NULL
        )
    , .level  = 0.95
    , .seed   = 9
    , .align  = TRUE
    , .model  = "AMMI"
    , .het    = FALSE
    , .waic   = TRUE
    )

2 Probabilities of superior performance and stability

Dias et al. (2022) select the best \(s\) fraction of \(G\) genotypes, that is \(\lceil sG \rceil\) of them, in every draw. Write \(\bar{\eta}_{i\cdot}\) for the mean of genotype \(i\) over the environments and \(\sigma^2_{GE,i} = \frac{1}{E}\sum_j(\eta_{ij} - \bar{\eta}_{i\cdot} - \bar{\eta}_{\cdot j} + \bar{\eta}_{\cdot\cdot})^2\) for its interaction variance. Then

  • the probability of superior performance is the share of draws in which \(\bar{\eta}_{i\cdot}\) is among the best \(\lceil sG \rceil\);
  • the probability of superior stability is the share in which \(\sigma^2_{GE,i}\) is among the smallest \(\lceil sG \rceil\);
  • the joint probability is their product;
  • the conditional probability is the probability of superior performance within each environment;
  • the pairwise probability is \(P(\bar{\eta}_{i\cdot} > \bar{\eta}_{i'\cdot})\).
sp <-
  bam_superiority(
      fit           = fit
    , .s            = 0.2
    , higher_better = TRUE
    )

4 Bayesian stability

bam_stability() gives the posterior of two stability measures for every genotype: the interaction variance of Shukla (1972), and the AMMI stability value of Purchase et al. (2000), \[\mathrm{ASV}_i = \sqrt{\left(\frac{\lambda_1}{\lambda_2}\,\lambda_1^{1/2}\alpha_{i1}\right)^2 + (\lambda_2^{1/2}\alpha_{i2})^2}.\] The weight \(\lambda_1/\lambda_2\) becomes unstable when \(\lambda_2\) is drawn near zero, so it is computed from the posterior means of \(\lambda_1\) and \(\lambda_2\), and the scores from each draw. The yield stability index (YSI) adds the mean rank of the yield to the mean rank of the ASV; small is good.

st <-
  bam_stability(
      fit           = fit
    , higher_better = TRUE
    )

7 Model choice

7.1 Information criteria

bayes_ammi() stores the deviance information criterion (DIC; Spiegelhalter et al. 2002) and the widely applicable information criterion (WAIC; Watanabe 2010) of the observed plots. Both are computed while the chains run, so no draws need to be kept. bam_compare() puts several fits side by side:

fits <-
  lapply(
      X   = 1:3
    , FUN = function(K) {
        bayes_ammi(
            .data   = Maiz
          , .y      = y
          , .gen    = entry
          , .env    = site
          , .rep    = rep
          , .nIter  = 2000
          , .k      = K
          , .burnin = 500
          , .thin   = 1
          , .chains = 1
          , .priors = bam_priors(
                mu_mean     = 0
              , mu_var      = 1e+8
              , tau_mean    = 0
              , tau_var     = 1e+8
              , delta_mean  = 0
              , delta_var   = 1e+8
              , lambda_mean = 0
              , lambda_var  = 1e+8
              , nu          = 1
              , s2          = NULL
              )
          , .level  = 0.95
          , .seed   = 9
          , .align  = TRUE
          , .model  = "AMMI"
          , .het    = TRUE
          , .waic   = TRUE
          )
      }
    )
names(fits) <- paste0("K", 1:3)
cp <- do.call(what = bam_compare, args = fits)

WAIC estimates the deviance of new plots of the same genotypes and environments (Gelman et al. 2014). se is the standard error of each WAIC; dWAIC_se is the standard error of the difference from the best model, computed from the plot-by-plot differences, and is the one to use when comparing (Vehtari et al. 2017). Here K2 has the smallest WAIC; K3 is 1.1 above it, 0.4 standard errors of the difference, and K1 is 13.5 above (2.1 standard errors). The column lambda_last_lower adds the posterior view: the HPD interval of the last singular value of K2 reaches down to 740, so a term whose interval nearly reaches zero improves prediction little and is weakly supported. Prefer the simplest model among those that the standard errors do not separate.

7.2 Posterior predictive checks

bam_ppc() simulates replicated trials from the posterior and compares statistics of the data with their replicated distribution (Gelman et al. 1996). A posterior predictive p-value near 0 or 1 marks a feature of the data the model does not reproduce.

pp <-
  bam_ppc(
      fit    = fit
    , .draws = 500
    , .seed  = 2
    )

Selecting 2 genotype(s): the highest joint probability of superior

- Probability of superior performance, largest first: 6 0.89, 5 0.63, 4 0.47;
  under 0.005: 9, 3, 1, 2, 7, 8.
- Probability of superior stability, largest first: 7 0.72, 3 0.72, 2 0.24, 6
  0.15, 5 0.10, 9 0.06, 1 0.02; under 0.005: 8, 4.

Recommendations:
- For a wide recommendation consider 6 and 5 (joint probability of at least
